bicheck 0.7.0 - find where your LDraw model differs from the LEGO building instructions
===============================================================================

LDCad tells you which parts you used too many or too few of. bicheck tells you WHERE:
the instructions step each wrong part belongs to, and the line in your model file.

HOW TO USE (Windows)
--------------------
1. Double-click bicheck.exe (or drag your model onto it). A small window opens.
2. Model: Browse to your model. Models can be LDraw (.ldr / .mpd, from LDCad, LeoCAD, ...)
   or BrickLink Studio (.io).
3. Set: bicheck fills in the set, read from the file or folder name, keywords or title
   (for example "21373  Downton Abbey"). Type another set number if it's wrong.
4. Instruction booklets: bicheck compares your model with the set's booklets, the PDFs from
   lego.com/service/building-instructions. Booklets it already has are listed. If there are
   none yet:
   - Paste links: on the LEGO page (Open LEGO page), right-click each booklet's Download
     button, Copy link, and paste them all: bicheck downloads them, or
   - Add PDFs: pick booklets you downloaded yourself, or
   - Brickset key: save your own free Brickset API key once (from
     brickset.com/tools/webservices/requestkey): bicheck then downloads the booklets of
     every set by itself.
   Booklets are kept in Documents\bicheck\booklets\<set number> (a folder beside your model
   with the set number in its name works too), so the next check finds them by itself.
5. Press Check model. The bar shows what it is doing. Reading the booklets takes about a
   minute for a big set, once: the result is kept. Then the report opens in your browser
   (Open the report opens it again). It is saved next to your model as
   <model name>.bicheck.html.
   (bicheck.exe --console asks these questions in the console window instead.)

Windows may warn that the program is from an unknown publisher (it isn't signed):
click "More info", then "Run anyway".

THE REPORT
----------
The top says how many parts to check, in how many steps, and how much of your model
lined up with the instructions.

"Mistakes by step": one card per booklet step (with its number as printed, the booklet
and the page), with pictures of the parts (drawn from your LDraw library, in their colours):
  - Wrong colour / Wrong part: your part first, marked with a red X ("Remove"), then the
    instructions' part with a green + ("Put in").
  - Swapped: the right part is in your model, but in another part's place close by
    (two parts traded places). The note says where it belongs.
  - Missing: a part of that step that isn't in your model.
  - Extra: a part in your model that isn't in the instructions.
  With the booklets, each card also shows the booklet page with the step number circled
  in red: click it to see the page large.
  The same mistake several times in a step is one row ("4 parts"). "Fix in your file"
  gives the submodel and the lines; "This step adds" shows the step's parts, to find
  the page in the booklet. A note says when a submodel is used more than once, so one
  fix covers several steps. The chips at the top turn small shifts, prints and
  minifigures on or off.

"Parts & colours": the same counts LDCad gives, as bars (instructions / your model), with
every step that uses each part and colour. Type in the search box, e.g. "3023 dark tan".

GOOD TO KNOW
------------
- With the booklets, bicheck compares step by step and part by part: the parts list at the
  back of the last booklet gives the totals, the parts box of each step says where a part
  goes, and each step of your model is matched to the booklet steps it holds the parts of.
  Your own steps don't need to follow the booklet. A right part in a wrong spot of its step
  can't be seen this way (the booklets have no positions).
- Part numbers are read from the booklets' element numbers with Rebrickable's element table
  (included). Only the downloads talk to the internet: the booklets come from lego.com, and
  with a Brickset key the set number and the key go to Brickset for the list of booklets.
  The key is saved in Documents\bicheck\settings.json.
- With an LXFML instructions file instead of the booklets (on the command line) bicheck
  lines your model up in 3D and also finds parts in the wrong spot and parts swapped with a
  neighbour ("Swapped"). Step numbers then count every step, sub-builds first, so they can
  differ from the booklet.
- Minifigures: their parts are listed under the "Minifigures" filter (off at first),
  since figures are often left out of models or built differently.
- Sections placed apart or turned at an angle (a car parked crooked) are lined up on
  their own. LDraw assemblies (e.g. 3829c01 steering wheel) are compared part by part.
- Prints: a plain part where the instructions have a print is listed separately
  ("Print and placement differences"), since LDraw lacks many prints.
- For the best part matching, have BrickLink Studio installed (bicheck reads its LEGO-to-
  LDraw part table). Part and colour names come from your LDraw library.

FROM THE COMMAND LINE
---------------------
  bicheck model.mpd                 (the set is read from the model, its booklets found)
  bicheck model.mpd booklet1.pdf booklet2.pdf
  bicheck model.mpd https://www.lego.com/cdn/product-assets/product.bi.core.pdf/6672240.pdf ...
  bicheck model.mpd --brickset-key YOUR-KEY            (saved: booklets download by themselves)
  bicheck model.mpd 21373
  bicheck model.mpd 21373 --find 3023 "dark tan"      (every step that uses a part)
  bicheck model.mpd instructions.lxfml --ldraw C:\LDraw
  bicheck --help
